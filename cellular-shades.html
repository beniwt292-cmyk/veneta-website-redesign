Cellular Shades

The warmest shade in the line.

Why cellular shades

Three jobs a honeycomb does that a flat shade cannot.

It works on the temperature, not just the light

The honeycomb traps a pocket of still air against the glass, which is where a house loses most of its heat. Double cell holds a second pocket and insulates best.

Blackout without a blackout look

A light filtering fabric reads as an even glow at midday. The same shade in blackout closes a bedroom down completely, with the same slim stack.

Top-down, bottom-up on the windows that need both

Drop the top rail for daylight with the street covered, or raise the bottom rail the usual way. One shade, both jobs.

Child & pet safety

No cord in reach.

Cordless lift is standard on this line, not a paid upgrade. Corded window coverings remain a strangulation hazard for young children, which is why the cordless option is the default here and not an option you have to find.

Guidance published by the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends cordless window coverings in any home with young children.

FAQ

If the answer you need is not here, the full list covers every line.

All questions
Do cellular shades really reduce heating and cooling costs?

They reduce heat transfer at the glass, which is where most homes lose the most energy. A double cell blackout shade insulates best. Savings depend on your window, orientation and climate, so we do not publish a single percentage.

Are these shades cordless?

Yes. Every cellular shade we make is cordless as standard. There is no looped cord anywhere on the product.

Can I motorize a cellular shade?

Yes, with TruQuiet™ motorization. Smart-home support depends on the motor and hub combination you choose, so check the compatibility list before you buy.

How do I clean them?

Dust with a soft brush attachment on low suction. Spot clean with a barely damp cloth and blot dry. Do not soak or submerge a blackout shade, because the liner can separate.

What is the minimum window depth?

3/4" for a flush inside mount. Anything shallower should use an outside mount, which we cover in the measuring guide.
