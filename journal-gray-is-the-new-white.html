Gray is the new white

Warm greys and greige have replaced stark white as the default neutral. Here is how to pick one that does not go blue.

January 22, 20264 min readVeneta editorial team

Why grey took over

Stark white reads cold under LED lighting and shows every mark. Warm grey and greige sit between white and beige, so they work with both cool and warm floors, which is exactly what an open-plan room needs.

The blue trap

Many greys have a blue undertone that only appears in north-facing daylight. On a swatch in a shop it looks neutral; on your window it looks cold. Always check the swatch on the actual window, at the time of day you use the room most.

A grey with a hint of brown in it will forgive almost any floor. A grey with a hint of blue will fight most of them.

Three combinations that work

  • Greige shades with warm oak floors and white trim.
  • Charcoal solar screens against dark metal window frames.
  • Fog grey cellular shades with a soft white wall for a low-contrast, quiet bedroom.

Where white still wins

Shutters. A framed shutter is architecture, and architecture usually wants to match the trim. Alabaster or pure white remains the right call in most houses.
