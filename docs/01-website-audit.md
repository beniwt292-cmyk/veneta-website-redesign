# VENETA™ Window Fashions — Full Website Audit

**Site audited:** https://www.venetawindowfashions.com/
**Date of audit:** August 13, 2026
**Platform detected:** WordPress with the Stockholm theme (v1.0.9), WPBakery Page Builder (v6.0.3), served through Cloudflare
**Business model:** Brand/catalog site. All purchasing happens on homedepot.com. Veneta is a Richfield Window Coverings brand.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The site works, but it reads as a 2019 brochure that has been patched year after year rather than redesigned. Three problems dominate everything else:

1. **It is not built to convert.** The whole reason this site exists is to send a shopper to a Home Depot product page or make them confident enough to buy custom blinds. There is no visible sticky "Shop at The Home Depot" action, no product finder, no swatch request path, no dealer/store locator, and no measurable funnel.
2. **It is technically heavy and structurally broken in places.** Roughly 4.7 MB and ~118 requests on the homepage, 73 separate JavaScript files (2.4 MB) and 25 stylesheets (1.4 MB). No `<h1>` on any page audited. Menu labels point to pages that return 404.
3. **The content contradicts itself and is stale.** The homepage promotes the ShadeAuto™ Hub with Alexa and Google Home compatibility, while the Cellular Shades FAQ states motorization "is not compatible with Alexa or any smart device." The footer still says © 2022. The blog's newest content is from around 2020-2021.

A redesign is justified. This should be a rebuild, not a reskin: the current theme, page builder, and slider stack are the source of most of the performance, responsive, and accessibility problems.

### Low / polish

- Typography uses default theme fonts with wide letter-spacing on body copy, and inconsistent centering.
- Headline colors (salmon/pink on the ShadeAuto hero) sit outside the brand palette established by the logo (black + yellow).
- Back-to-top button overlaps footer content.
- No favicon refresh, no Open Graph image tuned per page (16 OG tags exist but reuse a generic image).

- No visible accessibility statement despite a family-safety positioning.

## 3. Content audit

### What is genuinely good and must be preserved

- **The product specification content is excellent.** Cell sizes with min/max widths and heights, lift types, opacity explanations, mount types, and a deep, well-written FAQ set (including a full motorization FAQ). This is the site's real asset. Almost no competitor documents dimensions this clearly.
- Clear "how to measure" instructions with inside/outside mount logic.
- Distinct, trademarked innovations to build a story around: ClearFit™, SmartRail™, SmartPrivacy®, DualDrape™, TruQuiet™ Motorization, RevitaCharge™, ShadeAuto™ Hub, BelleVue™.
- Nine coherent product categories: Cellular, Roller & Solar, Roman, Faux Wood, Shutters, Sheer Shades, DualDrape™, Vertical Blinds (plus Cordless Roller and Cordless Roman sub-pages).

### What is wrong with the content

1. **Feature-led, not benefit-led.** The five value props ("Technical Innovation", "Family Safety", "High Quality", "Premium Materials", "Advanced Systems") are generic manufacturer language. They are interchangeable with any competitor and say nothing a shopper can act on.
2. **Written about the company, not the customer.** Nearly every paragraph starts with "Veneta™ has…" or "Veneta™ offers…".
3. **No decision support.** A shopper choosing custom blinds needs: which product for which room, which for privacy vs. light, blackout vs. light filtering, what it costs, how long it takes, how hard installation is, what happens if it does not fit. None of this is answered in a structured way.
4. **No pricing or lead-time signal at all.** Not even "from $X" or "ships in X days". This is the single biggest unanswered question and it is the top reason for drop-off.
5. **Copy duplication and copy errors** (see M9, M11).
6. **Stale claims.** "Award-winning" with no award named. "Latest designs" on a page last meaningfully updated years ago.
7. **Trademark symbols used inconsistently** and often mid-heading, which fragments headings into separate elements (e.g. "DualDrape" as `h2` and "™" as `h4`).
8. **The blog is abandoned** and its topics (spring cleaning, gray tones, 2020 color trends) are not tied to any product or conversion path.

## 4. UX and information architecture audit

Current top navigation: `HOME | PRODUCTS | INNOVATIVE FEATURES | DESIGN CENTER | INFORMATION | SHOP HOMEDEPOT.COM`

Problems:

- **"Home" in the nav wastes the most valuable slot** (the logo already does that job).
- **"Information" and "Design Center" are meaningless labels.** Users do not scan for abstractions; they scan for "How to Measure", "Inspiration", "Support".
- **Three of the six labels lead to 404s** when used as pages.
- **The Home Depot handoff is the last item and styled identically to everything else**, so the site's primary conversion looks like a footnote.
- **No breadcrumbs**, so deep pages (ClearFit™, SmartRail™) leave users stranded.
- **No cross-linking between products.** A shopper on Cellular is never shown Roller as an alternative.
- **The homepage carousel** (5+ auto-rotating slides) is a known low-engagement pattern; only the first slide gets meaningful attention and rotation actively harms accessibility.
- **The "Browse Our Latest Collections" grid** is a wall of 10 unlabeled room photos with a magnifier hover icon. Users cannot tell what product they are clicking.

## 7. Competitive context

The category leaders (Hunter Douglas, Levolor, Bali, Select Blinds, Blinds.com) all now offer: a guided product finder, room visualizer or AR preview, free swatch ordering in 2 clicks, transparent "from" pricing, verified reviews on every product, and a measure/install video library. Veneta currently offers none of these while owning better specification content than most of them.

**The strategic opening:** Veneta should not try to be a store (Home Depot is the store). It should be the most trustworthy *decision-making* site in the category — the place where you figure out exactly what to buy, then click through to buy it.

## 8. Prioritized recommendations

**Tier 1 — Do first (fixes conversion and credibility)**
1. Rebuild on a modern stack (Next.js + headless CMS or a lean custom WordPress theme); retire Stockholm + WPBakery + slider plugins.
2. Add a sticky "Shop at The Home Depot" primary action and track every outbound click as a conversion event.
3. Build a **Product Finder** (Room → Need → Look → 2-3 recommended products).
4. Rewrite all headings semantically: one `h1` per page, body copy as `<p>`.
5. Resolve the smart-home contradiction with one source of truth for compatibility.
6. Fix the navigation: replace 404 labels with real pages, add a store locator or "Where to Buy" page.
7. Replace image-baked headlines with real text over background images.

**Tier 2 — Content system**
8. Rewrite all nine product pages to a single benefit-led template with visible (not tabbed) sections and anchor navigation.
9. Add "From $XX" price signals and lead times, even as ranges.
10. Add reviews/ratings (syndicate from Home Depot if licensing allows) and real customer photos.
11. Build a real "Measure, Install & Care" hub with short videos and printable PDFs.
12. Restart the blog as an inspiration/guides hub tied to products, with a publishing calendar.

**Tier 3 — Growth and polish**
13. Product, FAQ, Organization, and Breadcrumb structured data across the site.
14. Room visualizer or before/after slider per product.
15. Swatch-request flow with clear expectations.
16. Trade/pro section (builders, designers, property managers) with spec sheets and a spec-book download.
17. Full WCAG 2.2 AA remediation with an accessibility statement.
18. Analytics: GA4 + Search Console + outbound click events + scroll/engagement on product pages.
