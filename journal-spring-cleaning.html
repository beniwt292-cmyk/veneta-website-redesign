Care

Spring cleaning for your window treatments

A material-by-material routine that takes an afternoon and buys you years of good-looking blinds.

March 4, 20266 min readVeneta editorial team

Start with dust, not water

Almost every window treatment problem starts as dust that got damp. Dry dust brushes off; damp dust becomes a stain that needs professional cleaning. So the order matters: dust everything first, then decide what genuinely needs a wet clean.

Use a vacuum brush attachment on the lowest suction. Work top to bottom so you are not re-dusting what you already did.

Fabric shades: cellular, Roman and sheer

  • Brush with the grain of the fabric, never across it.
  • Spot clean with a barely damp white cloth and blot, do not rub.
  • Never soak a blackout shade. The foil liner can separate from the face fabric.
  • For anything larger than a spot, use a professional cleaner who handles window coverings.

Faux wood and vinyl

This is the easy category. Close the slats one way, wipe with a damp microfibre cloth, then flip and do the other side. A drop of dish soap in warm water handles kitchen grease. Skip furniture polish, which leaves a film that attracts dust.

Shutters

Dust the louvres closed, in one direction, then reverse them. Pay attention to the frame corners where dust collects. On a painted finish, avoid any solvent cleaner.

The 15-minute maintenance check

  • Raise and lower every shade fully once. Anything that binds needs attention now, not in July.
  • Check that brackets are still tight. Seasonal movement loosens screws.
  • Look at the bottom hem for curl or fraying.
  • Confirm no cords, cables or chains have appeared within a child's reach.

Every Veneta product carries a limited lifetime warranty. If something is not working the way it should after a clean, contact support before you try to repair it yourself.
