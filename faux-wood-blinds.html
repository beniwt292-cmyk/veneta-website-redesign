Why faux wood blinds

Built for the rooms that ruin real wood.

Wood grain that will not move

A polymer composite takes a real grain finish and then ignores the steam from a shower or a kettle. No warping, no bowing, no yellowing at the bottom slat.

Tilt is the real control

Slats aimed up bounce daylight onto the ceiling. Aimed down they hold privacy and keep the view of the ground. That is a different tool from raising a shade.

Routeless slats close the pinholes

The standard slat carries a route hole at every ladder. The routeless option moves the ladder to the edge, so a closed blind reads as a solid surface.

Child & pet safety

No cord in reach.

Cordless lift is standard on this line, not a paid upgrade. Corded window coverings remain a strangulation hazard for young children, which is why the cordless option is the default here and not an option you have to find.

Guidance published by the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends cordless window coverings in any home with young children.

FAQ

Asked often, answered plainly.

If the answer you need is not here, the full list covers every line.

All questions
Will faux wood warp in a bathroom?

No. The composite is specified for humid rooms, which is the main reason to choose it over real wood. Avoid direct standing water on the slats.

What is routeless and is it worth it?

Routeless slats have no lift-cord holes, so you do not get the row of pinholes of light down the blind. On a bedroom or street-facing window, yes, it is worth it.

How heavy are wide faux wood blinds?

Heavier than aluminium or fabric. Above 72" wide we recommend either motorization or splitting the window into two blinds on one headrail.

Can I cut the slats to fit myself?

Please do not. Every blind is built to your measurements. If a blind arrives wrong, contact us and we will make it right under the warranty.
