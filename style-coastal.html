Style · Coastal

Bright, salt-washed, and never actually blue.

Coastal rooms are about light and air, not nautical colour. The look is built on pale warm neutrals with the view kept open, and at most one blue in the whole room.

Four rules

How the look is actually built.

Style advice is usually a mood board. These are four decisions you can make at the point of ordering.

01

Keep the view, always

A coastal room is bought for what is outside it. Specify a 10% or 14% screen or a sheer vane, and treat a fully opaque shade as a bedroom-only decision.

02

Pale and warm, not pale and cold

Chalk, Oyster and Snow. A cool grey in a bright coastal light goes flat and slightly blue, which is not the blue anyone wants.

03

One blue, used once

Bellevue Blue or Coastal Blue on a single window or a single room. Repeated across a house it stops being a colour and becomes a theme.

04

Composite where the air is salt

Near the coast, humidity and salt are constant. Faux wood and composite shutters are the materials that do not care about either.

Three lines that carry this look.

Product 01

Roller & Solar.

A 10% or 14% screen in Chalk or Oyster keeps the water in the window and takes the hard edge off a very bright light. This is the coastal workhorse.

Product 02

Sheer Shades.

Straight answer

What to avoid.

The fastest way to break a look is one element that belongs to a different one.

Navy across a whole house, rope and shell detailing, dark screen fabric on a water view, and real wood anywhere near salt air. Also: blackout on the main living window, which throws away the reason the room exists.
