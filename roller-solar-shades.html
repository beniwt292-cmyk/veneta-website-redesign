Clean lines. Precise light control.

Why roller & solar

One flat fabric, and the whole decision is openness.

Openness is a number, so choose it deliberately

1% blocks the most heat and glare and gives the least view. 14% keeps the view and lets more sun through. 5% is the default for a west-facing room.

One clean sweep of fabric

No folds, no slats, no stack lines. Lowered it is a flat plane, raised it disappears into a 2" roll at the top of the opening.

Blackout on the same hardware

A blackout roller uses the same bracket and bottom bar as the screen, so a bedroom and an office can read as one system across a facade.

Child & pet safety

No cord in reach.

Cordless lift is standard on this line, not a paid upgrade. Corded window coverings remain a strangulation hazard for young children, which is why the cordless option is the default here and not an option you have to find.

Guidance published by the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends cordless window coverings in any home with young children.

FAQ

Asked often, answered plainly.

If the answer you need is not here, the full list covers every line.

All questions
Which openness factor should I choose?

Start with the problem. Glare on a screen: 1% or 3%. Heat on a west window: 3% or 5%. A view you want to keep: 10% or 14%. Openness does not equal privacy at night, because a lit room is visible through any screen fabric.

Will a solar shade give me privacy after dark?

No. Screen fabrics reverse at night: with the lights on inside, you are visible from outside. Pair a solar screen with a blackout roller or a drapery panel if you need night privacy.

What is reverse roll?

The fabric comes off the front of the tube instead of the back. Choose it when a deep window frame or a crank handle would otherwise sit in the shade's path.

Can two shades share one headrail?

Yes, on widths above 48" we can build a dual bracket that carries a solar screen and a blackout roller on the same window.
