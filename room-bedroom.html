Room · Bedroom

Dark enough to sleep. Quiet enough to stay asleep.

A bedroom asks for two things a living room does not: real darkness on demand, and a bit less of the street. One product line does both better than the rest.

Bedroom

What this room actually demands.

The brief

Darkness first, sound second, look third. The order matters, because the products that do the first two well are all fabric, and fabric is where the design decisions are.

Where bedrooms go wrong

People buy blackout fabric and stop there. The fabric was never the problem: the gap down each side of an inside-mounted shade is, and on a summer morning it throws a line of light straight across the bed.

The quiet part

A double cell honeycomb has two air pockets, and still air absorbs reflected sound as well as heat. It will not silence a main road, and we would not claim it does, but it audibly takes the edge off traffic and reflected noise.

What we would fit

Three that suit this room.

Ranked, with the downside written next to each one.

Pick 01 · Cellular Shades

Double cell blackout, with side channels

The bedroom default. Darkest fabric face we make, two air pockets for warmth and sound, and SmartPrivacy® channels to close the sides. Add top-down/bottom-up on a street-facing window for daylight above head height.

The tradeoff. The channels are visible and have to be measured in from the start. On an opening shallower than 3/4" you are going to an outside mount instead.

Cellular Shades

Pick 02 · Roller & Solar

Blackout roller with a fascia

The cleanest look: one flat panel, nothing stacked at the top when it is up. A fascia or cassette closes the light path above the tube, which is the second-biggest leak after the sides.

The tradeoff. Edge leak is worse than a channelled cellular shade. Plan a generous outside mount if the room has to be genuinely dark.

Roller & Solar

Pick 03 · Roman Shades

Blackout-lined, in a fabric you like

For a bedroom that should not look technical. Flat fold in a linen or a hobbled fold for something softer, with a blackout lining behind it doing the work.

The tradeoff. Folds break the seal at the bottom and the stack never fully clears the glass. Reliably dim, not reliably dark, and dry clean only.

Measuring notes

Four things specific to this room.

The general method is in the measuring guide. These are the ones people get wrong here.

01

Check depth before anything else

A flush inside mount needs 3/4" for cellular and 2 1/2" for a roman. Measure to the face of the glass, not to the trim.

02

Measure in three places

Width at top, middle and bottom; height at left, centre and right. Use the narrowest width and the longest height. Old bedrooms are rarely square.

03

Decide the channel now, not later

SmartPrivacy® channels change the ordered width. They cannot be added to a shade that was cut for a bare opening.

04

Two windows, one fabric

If the room has two windows, order both at once from the same dye lot and hold the swatches side by side under the bedroom's own light.

Straight answer

What to avoid in this room.

The range has eight lines. Some of them are the wrong answer here, and it is cheaper to read that than to return an order.

  • Sheer shades, if daytime sleep matters. Lovely product, wrong room for this brief.
  • Faux wood or shutters as the only treatment. Closed louvres still pass a hairline of light, and at 5am in June that is enough to wake you.
  • A light filtering fabric on a street-facing window. It silhouettes at night, which is the one room where that is genuinely unwelcome.

Questions

Asked and answered.

What is the best blind for a bedroom?

A double cell blackout cellular shade with SmartPrivacy® side channels. It is the darkest, warmest and quietest combination in the range, and cordless lift is standard so there is nothing to fit around a bed.

How do I stop light coming in around the edges?

Side channels if you want it solved properly, or an outside mount with at least two inches of overlap each side and above if you do not want a visible frame. The blackout page goes through both.

Will shades help with street noise?

A double cell honeycomb will noticeably soften reflected and mid-frequency noise. It will not stop a bus. Glazing does that, and no window covering is a substitute for it.

Are motorised bedroom shades worth it?

On a high window or behind a bed you cannot reach across, yes. TruQuiet™ is rechargeable lithium and quiet enough not to wake the room, which is the whole point of the name.

Can I have blackout and still get daylight in the morning?

Top-down/bottom-up on a cellular shade gives you exactly that: leave the bottom closed and drop the top rail a foot. Light arrives at the ceiling, the bed stays in shade, and nobody can see in.
