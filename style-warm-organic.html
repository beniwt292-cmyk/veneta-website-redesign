Style · Warm organic

Texture doing the work that colour usually does.

A warm organic room is nearly monochrome and never flat. The interest comes from weave, slub and hand, which is a specification you cannot judge on a screen.

See the paletteInspiration gallery

Four rules

How the look is actually built.

Style advice is usually a mood board. These are four decisions you can make at the point of ordering.

01

Choose weave over pattern

An open flax weave changes through the day as the light moves across it. A printed pattern looks the same at every hour.

02

Stay inside one temperature

Everything warm, or everything cool. A greige next to a cool grey reads as a mistake even when both are beautiful alone.

03

Let it fold

This is the one look where a roman shade is the right first answer. The fold is what shows the fabric has weight.

04

Order the swatches, genuinely

Hand and slub are the entire point and no screen transmits either. Order eight, and look at them at the hour you use the room.

Products

Three lines that carry this look.

Product 01

Roman Shades.

A flat fold in an unlined linen is the centre of this look. Hobbled fold if you want it softer still; add a privacy or blackout lining and the texture stays on the room side.

Roman Shades

Product 02

Sheer Shades.

Knit facings over a fabric vane, which is texture and light control in the same product. Almond and Taupe sit exactly in this palette.

Sheer Shades

Product 03

Cellular Shades.

The practical member of the group. In Sand or Linen it holds the temperature of the room in both senses, on the windows where a roman would be impractical.

Cellular Shades

Straight answer

What to avoid.

The fastest way to break a look is one element that belongs to a different one.

Cool greys, bright whites, high-sheen fabrics and vinyl. Also: a heavily textured fabric on a window with no direct light, where the weave never catches and you have paid for texture you cannot see.
