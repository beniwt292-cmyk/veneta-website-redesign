Design

Faux wood blinds do more for your decor than you think

Wide slats, the right white and a matched valance turn a budget blind into trim that looks built in.

February 11, 20265 min readVeneta editorial team

The slat width sets the tone

A 2" slat is quiet and standard. A 2 1/2" slat changes the character of the window: fewer lines across the glass, a wider view when tilted open, and a more architectural read from across the room. On a large window, wider almost always looks more expensive.

Match the trim, not the wall

The most common mistake is matching the blind to the wall colour. Match the trim instead. When the blind, the casing and the baseboard share a white, the window stops looking like it has a product hanging in it and starts looking finished.

Bright white next to antique white trim looks like a mistake from ten feet away. Order the swatch.

Use the valance

The included crown valance hides the headrail. Skipping it saves nothing and leaves the mechanism on show. Install it.

Where faux wood beats real wood

  • Kitchens, bathrooms and laundry rooms, because humidity warps real wood.
  • South and west windows, where heat cycles move solid timber.
  • Wide windows, where composite costs meaningfully less at the same slat size.

And where it does not

On a very tall window, weight becomes the issue. Above 72" wide, either motorize the blind or split the opening into two blinds on one headrail.
